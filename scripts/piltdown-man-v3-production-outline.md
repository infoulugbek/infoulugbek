## How to use this outline

- **Durations are estimates, not timecodes.** Each one is the narration at 150 words per minute, plus any planned pauses, given as a range. Precise timecodes come only after the voiceover is recorded and transcribed (see the hand-off to `/yt-edit` at the end).
- **Claim IDs** (C01…) and **source keys** (DG16, NHM-1…) refer to the claims ledger and source table in the script.
- **Picture sources** are suggestions. Every archival asset needs its license cleared before use (U8).
- **Recreated props** (letters, postcards, newspapers) must not be passed off as originals. Label them on screen or in the description.

### S03 · The collector  (PART 1: BUILT)

**Estimated duration:** ~38–43 s (90 words)

**Narration**

> Piltdown is a village about forty miles south of London. In 1912, it was home turf for a lawyer named Charles Dawson.
> Dawson was an amateur collector with a remarkable run of luck: fossils, antiquities, one find after another. A local paper called him "the Wizard of Sussex."
> What he didn't have was standing with the professionals.
> On February 14, 1912, he wrote to Arthur Smith Woodward, Keeper of Geology at London's Natural History Museum. Dawson said he had found part of a thick human skull in a gravel pit.

**Picture (from the script)**

- Drone over East Sussex fields. Map graphic: London, then a pin drops on Piltdown, near Uckfield.
- Portrait of Dawson. A slow pan across cabinets of flints, coins and fossils.
- A handwritten letter. The date resolves: 14th February 1912.

**Shot plan:** Aerial of the East Sussex fields, map animation, portrait, collection cabinets, the 1912 letter.

**Picture sources:** Aerial: stock or licensed drone footage of the Piltdown area. Dawson portrait: NHM / Mary Evans. Letter: a recreated prop, labeled as a reconstruction unless the original is licensed (NHM archives).

**Notes:** Map style set here and reused in S06, S07, S16, S19.

**Fact sources:** C03, C04, C05. See the claims ledger.

### S11 · The cost  (PART 2: SURVIVED)

**Estimated duration:** ~47–53 s (109 words)

**Narration**

> The damage spread beyond Britain. In 1924, the anatomist Raymond Dart in Johannesburg received a fossil skull from a quarry at Taung. It was a child's: an apelike brain, with teeth that looked surprisingly human.
> Dart argued it was a human relative, and that our story began in Africa. Leading British experts pushed back.
> Put the two side by side and you can see the problem. Taung was Piltdown in reverse. If one was right, the other looked wrong.
> Dart's view didn't win wide acceptance until the late 1940s.
> In 1938, a memorial stone was unveiled at Barkham Manor, marking where the dawn man was found. It's still there.

**Picture (from the script)**

- The February 7, 1925 issue of *Nature*: "*Australopithecus africanus*: The Man-Ape of South Africa."
- Split screen. Taung, "small brain, human teeth". Piltdown, "big brain, ape jaw".
- The Piltdown memorial stone at Barkham Manor, gray in long grass.

**Shot plan:** South African archival footage, Taung skull, *Nature* 1925 page, split-screen comparison, memorial stone.

**Picture sources:** Taung: University of the Witwatersrand image licensing. *Nature* page: Springer Nature permissions. Memorial: original footage at Barkham Manor (private land; get permission) or licensed photo.

### S12 · The first crack  (PART 3: BROKEN)

**Estimated duration:** ~30–32 s (70 words)

**Narration**

> The first crack came from chemistry.
> Buried bone slowly absorbs fluorine from groundwater. The longer it lies in the ground, the more it soaks up. In 1949, Kenneth Oakley of the Natural History Museum tested Piltdown.
> The animal fossils were genuinely ancient. The dawn man was not. He was far younger than the animals he was supposed to have lived alongside.
> A puzzle. But not yet proof of a fake.

**Picture (from the script)**

- A 1940s laboratory. A man in a white coat drills a pinch of powder from a bone.
- Animated bar chart. The animal fossils' bars rise tall. The skull and jaw barely leave the baseline.

**Shot plan:** 1940s lab reconstruction; animated fluorine bar chart.

**Picture sources:** Lab: reconstruction or stock. Chart: motion graphic. Use no numbers, only relative bars (see U1 and C27).

**Notes:** The chart shows only relative levels (old versus young), no figures.

**Fact sources:** C27. See the claims ledger.

### S16 · The answer to the hook  (PART 3: BROKEN)

**Estimated duration:** ~58–62 s (140 words)

**Narration**

> The jaw came from an orangutan. Its joint with the skull had been broken away, so nobody could check whether the two pieces actually fit.
> On November 21, 1953, the three scientists published their findings. Piltdown Man was a forgery.
> Follow-up work went further. The skull was human, and nowhere near as old as claimed. It's usually described as medieval, though attempts to date it precisely haven't settled the question.
> Even the animal fossils had been brought in. A hippo tooth probably came from Malta. An elephant tooth, probably from Tunisia. And the "cricket bat" had been shaped with a steel knife.
> So that's why the doubters couldn't prove it. The proof wasn't in what the bones looked like. It was in what had been done to them, and that took chemistry, a microscope and someone willing to suspect fraud.

**Picture (from the script)**

- The reconstruction comes apart: braincase one way, jaw the other. Labels: "HUMAN" and "ORANGUTAN".
- A November 1953 front page announcing the forgery.
- A map. Lines run from the Mediterranean to Sussex.

**Shot plan:** Reconstruction comes apart, labels; 1953 front page; Mediterranean map.

**Picture sources:** Reuse the S05 3D asset. Front page: British Newspaper Archive / licensed archive.

**Notes:** The hook's answer line must land on a clean, quiet shot.

**Fact sources:** C32, C33, C34, C35, C36. See the claims ledger.

## Hand-off to `/yt-edit`

`/yt-edit` works on a **timestamped transcript of a recorded take** (.srt, .vtt or Whisper JSON) and outputs cuts for dead air, filler and retakes. It can't run on this script. To get there:

1. Record the narration from the v3 script (S01–S21, in order, with a 2-second room-tone gap between scenes).
2. Transcribe it with timestamps (`whisper` / `faster-whisper`), giving `narration.srt`.
3. Run `python3 .claude/skills/yt-edit/deadair.py narration.srt`. Check its suggestions against the planned silences (S10, after S13's question, the S15 reveal), and don't cut those.
4. Replace the duration ranges in this outline with the real timecodes from the cleaned take, then cut the picture to them.
